# Field Visit Notes: TRI Sub Centre, Ratnapura

**Date:** 04/05/2026  
**Purpose:** Primary data collection for project – observe manual vs. mechanical tea harvesting, identify limitations of existing systems and understand tea shoot morphology.  
**Attendees:** 
* E/21/097 Dilhara
* E/21/099 Dilshan
* E/21/218 Kaushalya

**TRI Contact:** Dr. S.R.W. Pathiranage (Head of low country regional centre / Senior research officer)

## Key Observations

### 1. Manual Plucking
* **High Selectivity:** Workers consistently pick "2–3 leaves and a bud".
* **Arimbu Conservation:** *Arimbu* (immature shoots) are left intentionally for the next plucking round (7 days).
* **Labor Output:** Skilled pluckers achieve 15–20 kg/day, but labor shortages are highly visible.

### 2. Mechanical Harvesters (Kawasaki NV60H)
* **Non-Selective Cutting:** Removes buds, coarse leaves, stems, and *Arimbu* all together.
* **Yield Loss:** Estimated 35–40% yield loss due to the accidental removal of immature shoots.
* **Biological Damage:** Damage to maintenance foliage (mother leaves) severely affects future photosynthesis.
* **Terrain Constraints:** On steep slopes, operators struggle to maintain a constant, stable cutting height.

### 3. Tea Shoot Structure
* **Morphology:** Apical bud, fish leaf, and mother leaf were clearly identified during the field observation with the TRI officer.
* **Banji Shoots:** *Banji* shoots (dormant buds) are present in dry patches, which current non-selective machines cannot detect.
* **Plucking Table:** The plucking table height is highly uneven due to the rugged terrain, causing over-cutting or completely missed shoots.

### 4. TRI Selective Harvesting Shear
* **Quality & Speed:** Provides much better quality (2× faster than hand plucking with good selectivity).
* **Limitations:** Displays low overall productivity (4.3 kg/hr) and causes severe hand fatigue on slopes. It is currently not suitable for large-scale application.
