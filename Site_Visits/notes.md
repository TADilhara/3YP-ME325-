Field Visit Notes: TRI Sub Centre, Ratnapura

Date: 04/05/2026

Purpose: Primary data collection for project – observe manual vs. mechanical tea harvesting, identify limitations of existing systems and understand tea shoot morphology.

Attendees: E/21/097 Dilhara, E/21/099 Dilshan, E/21/218 Kaushalya

TRI contact: Dr. S.R.W. Pathiranage (Head of low country regional centre/ Senior research officer)

Key Observations
1. Manual plucking:
   - High selectivity: workers consistently pick "2-3 leaves and a bud"
   - Arimbu (immature shoots) left intentionally for next plucking round (7 days)
   - Skilled pluckers achieve 15–20 kg/day, but labour shortage visible

2. Mechanical harvesters (Kawasaki NV60H):
   - Non-selective cutting: removes buds, coarse leaves, stems and Arimbu together
   - Estimated 35–40% yield loss due to immature shoot removal
   - Damage to maintenance foliage (mother leaves) – affects future photosynthesis
   - On steep slopes, operators struggle to maintain constant cutting height

3. Tea shoot structure (observed in field with TRI officer):
   - Apical bud, fish leaf, mother leaf clearly identified
   - Banji shoots (dormant buds) present in dry patches – machines cannot detect them
   - Plucking table height uneven due to terrain – causes over-cutting or missed shoots

4. TRI selective harvesting shear:
   - Much better quality (2× faster than hand plucking, good selectivity)
   - But low productivity (4.3 kg/hr) and causes hand fatigue on slopes
   - Not suitable for large-scale use
