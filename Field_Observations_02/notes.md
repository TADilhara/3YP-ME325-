## Key Observations & Data Collection

### 1. Custom Bending Force Apparatus
To determine the stiffness boundary conditions for our selective mechanism design, our team deployed a custom mechanical testing apparatus directly in the field patches.
* **Component Assembly:** The system utilizes a rigid structural clamp to secure the base of the living tea stem without crushing the plant tissue. A low-friction pulley wheel with an integrated holder is aligned horizontally with the target pluck point via a high-tensile thread link. The thread terminates at a calibrated scale pan used to apply incremental vertical loads.
* **Testing Methodology:** Force application is managed using calibrated laboratory weights supplemented by a precise combination of Sri Lankan currency coins (with known masses verified in our coin-weight lookup tables) to achieve fractional gram sensitivities. Weights are added slowly until the stalk reaches its critical large-deflection bending threshold, allowing us to back-calculate the mechanical yield limits in Newtons ($F = m \cdot g$).

### 2. Shoot Bending Force & Stem Diameter Metrics
Using our custom testing apparatus and precision vernier calipers, we successfully isolated the distinct mechanical properties separating immature, harvestable, and over-mature stalks:
* **Arimbu (Immature Stems):** Displayed a highly flexible biological nature with a narrow stem diameter. The maximum force threshold required to bend an *Arimbu* shoot tip safely sat at **0.1089 N**, setting our mechanism's absolute baseline resistance limit.
* **Harvestable Shoots (Pluckable):** Standard "2-3 leaves and a bud" structures showed a clean nominal diameter profile at their natural breaking points. These shoots displayed a clear force range starting from a minimum of **0.1178 N** up to a maximum peak of **0.3226 N**.
* **Mature Shoots (Coarse Wood Stalks):** Heavily lignified, dense mature stems exhibited significantly larger cross-sectional diameters, requiring extreme forces exceeding **0.50 N - 0.70 N** to achieve deflection, validating our stiffness-sorting criteria.

### 3. Field Agronomy & Structural Observations
* **Manual Harvesting Baseline:** Accompanied by the TRI officers, we monitored plantation pluckers to study the kinematic motion of hand selection. Experienced labor isolates pluckable shoots with high accuracy, leaving delicate *Arimbu* tips pristine for subsequent 7-day or 9-day cycles.
* **Morphological Features:** Detailed structural evaluations confirmed that the mechanical layout must adapt to variable stalk maturity across uneven tea tables, confirming that a geometry-only sorting method is insufficient compared to our stiffness-gate approach.

### 4. Commercial Tea Factory Processing Pipeline
* **Industrial Validation:** Visited the manufacturing plant facilities to analyze how mechanical harvesting affects subsequent processing stages.
* **Coarse Leaf Impact:** Factory operators confirmed that non-selective machine harvesting introduces high percentages of coarse wood stems, which disrupts the automated sifting/withering machines and degrades the overall value grade of the final product.
